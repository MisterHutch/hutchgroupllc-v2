# Hutchgroup market and revenue direction — October 16, 2026

**Status:** Research-backed operating hypothesis. This document does not establish local demand, customer outcomes, or a revenue forecast. Site changes in this branch are draft until released and checked in production.

## Decision

Lead with a **measured lead handoff** for Twin Cities service businesses. Start with the existing [Lead Flow Starter](https://hutchgroupllc.com/lead-flow-starter/): one inquiry path, one responsible owner, one follow-up behavior, a tested handoff, and a baseline. Use the free Opportunity Review to qualify the problem. Offer the broader AI Demand Capture Pilot and ongoing support only when discovery and the first handoff justify them. Keep [Hutchgroup Ops](https://ops.hutchgroupllc.com/) as an interactive demonstration with synthetic data, not customer proof.

This matches Hutch's stated direction: become the Twin Cities small-business partner for market visibility and consolidated sales leads, with Ops rather than Prep as the public product proof. It also gives a buyer a small, concrete first purchase instead of asking them to select a stack of AI tools.

## Where AI fits as the tools advance

[Microsoft's 2026 Work Trend Index](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization) describes a shift toward agent-assisted workflows with people setting direction, judging output, and owning results. Its respondents were AI-using workers, not a representative sample of Twin Cities small-business buyers, so use it as a design signal rather than a demand estimate. The more durable service for Hutchgroup is to connect a customer's inquiry sources, approved business knowledge, owner assignments, and outcome records so different AI tools can assist the workflow without becoming its source of truth.

For any later agent-assisted step, specify the exact allowed task, approved answers and data, human escalation, failed-response handling, and a metric tied to the original handoff. Start with reply drafts, summaries, or triage when they reduce a measured delay; require a person to approve customer-facing commitments until the buyer has accepted a tested workflow. Recheck provider features and costs for each proposal. This is the practical route from today's Starter to a broader pilot without promising a platform capability the customer has not bought or approved.

## Commercial ladder to test

1. **Opportunity Review:** Free 15-minute conversation; identify the current inquiry path, volume, ownership, cost of delay, and the buyer's authority to change it. A review request is not a booked meeting.
2. **Lead Flow Starter:** Proposed starting price around $1,500 for one scoped path, ownership/notification rule, follow-up behavior, test, baseline, and handover. Confirm price after discovery; subscriptions, SMS, ads, and unusual integrations are separate.
3. **AI Demand Capture Pilot:** Existing proposed starting point around $3,500 when multiple channels or approved AI-assisted steps are genuinely needed. State the actual provider capabilities, data access, human review, and measurement in the proposal.
4. **Ongoing improvement:** Quote support only after a working implementation and a useful review cadence exist. No recurring revenue is counted until a customer signs and pays.

**Planning illustration, not forecast:** Three $1,500 starters and one $3,500 pilot would book $8,000 in project revenue before labor, software, acquisition cost, taxes, and collections. Those four wins have not been observed. Track delivery hours and pass-through costs before deciding whether the price is viable. Avoid extrapolating from national AI adoption to revenue.

At the proposed $1,500 Starter price, 12 delivery hours would produce $125 of **revenue per delivery hour**; 20 hours would produce $75. Both figures exclude sales time, software, overhead, tax, and collection risk. This makes a tight scope and time log part of the offer test, not a later administrative detail. No margin or founder pay rate is established yet.

**First-month activity gates, not projected wins:** Aim to learn from 10 relevant owner or partner conversations, obtain 3 qualified Reviews, prepare 2 scoped proposals, and test whether 1 Starter can be sold. If conversations are irrelevant, narrow the audience or channel. If qualified buyers decline proposals, record whether scope, price, urgency, or trust caused the loss. If a Starter sells but needs more than the planned hours, fix the scope before repeating it. Record actual counts, including zero, rather than reporting these targets as results.

## Website direction and next decisions

- Preserve the current homepage sequence: Twin Cities visibility, consolidated leads, follow-up, and Ops demo. Put the Lead Flow Starter first in the path to a paid engagement; let the digital menu explain expansion after discovery. Keep practical AI tied to a specific approved workflow and human oversight.
- Keep the existing roofing and HVAC pages as hypotheses until Search Console queries, buyer interviews, and paid opportunities indicate which vertical deserves more resources. Do not create more location or industry pages merely to fill the sitemap.
- Use one primary conversion definition: **qualified Review conversation**. Also track review requests, booked conversations, proposals, paid starts, revenue collected, and delivery hours separately. Add source/medium and campaign attribution only through the site's allowlist, without contact or calculator inputs in analytics.
- Maintain an owner-reviewed learning record after each meaningful experiment: what changed, why, how, evidence, what worked, what did not, Hutch's directly stated preference versus inferred tone, and the next test. The reviewed event is synced nightly to Honcho; a timer cannot infer lessons from conversations on its own.
