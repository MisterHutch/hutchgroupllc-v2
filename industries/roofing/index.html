Where an estimate loses momentum

The useful first step is to map the way your team actually works, from the initial request through the customer's decision. These are common places to check, not claims about your business.

Inquiry to inspection

Who schedules the visit?

A web form, call, or referral needs a clear owner and an appointment status the team can find.

Inspection to estimate

What was promised?

Keep the scope questions, estimate owner, expected send date, and customer update together.

Estimate to decision

When do we follow up?

Record that an estimate was sent, the next contact date, and any question that needs an answer.

A practical roofing handoff

This example is an illustrative process, not a measured customer result. Your team would adapt the stages to the kind of work it performs.

  1. Record the request. Note the source, property or service area, reason for contact, and the person responsible for the reply.
  2. Confirm the inspection. Give the scheduler and estimator a shared status so the customer does not have to repeat the same information.
  3. Track the estimate. Show whether the scope is being prepared, sent, revised, approved, or declined.
  4. Assign the follow-up. Put the next conversation and due date on the opportunity, then close the loop when a decision is known.

Start by measuring the share of inquiries with an owner, time to first response, estimates with a scheduled next action, and booked work. Establish the baseline before making improvement claims.

Questions roofing owners ask

Do we need a new CRM?

We first inspect the systems you already use. A clearer handoff, form, or reminder may solve the immediate gap. An integration or new system is a scoped decision.

Can this include calls and referrals?

Yes. The workflow can include phone, website, email, and referral inquiries. We define how each enters a shared record and who owns the response.

Will this send automatic follow-up messages?

That depends on your approved process and tools. We scope message content, timing, consent, and human review before automating any outbound contact.
